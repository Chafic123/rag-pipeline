What is retrieval-augmented generation?

Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, makes a language model answer using your own documents instead of only its training data. First the documents are split into chunks, and each chunk is turned into an embedding. When a question arrives, the system embeds the question, retrieves the chunks most similar to it, and passes them to the model as context. The model then generates an answer grounded in those chunks. Chunk size matters: chunks that are too large dilute the relevant sentence, and chunks that are too small lose surrounding meaning, so a common choice is a few hundred characters with some overlap between neighbours. The instruction usually tells the model to use only the provided context and to say it does not know when the answer is absent. This grounding is what stops the model from inventing facts.
