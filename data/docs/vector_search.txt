How does vector search work?

An embedding is a list of numbers that captures the meaning of a piece of text, so that texts with similar meaning sit close together in vector space. Similarity is measured with cosine similarity, the cosine of the angle between two vectors, which ignores their length and looks only at direction. Comparing a query against every stored vector is called flat or brute-force search and costs O(n), which is too slow for millions of vectors. Approximate nearest neighbour search trades a little accuracy for speed. The most common method is HNSW, hierarchical navigable small world, which links each vector to its nearest neighbours and adds sparse upper layers so a search takes long hops first and short hops near the end. It visits a few hundred vectors instead of millions.
